Prestige Bannerghatta Road residential plots

A residential plot at Prestige Bannerghatta Road is a piece of land in Prestige’s layout on which you build a home of your own. You choose the architect, the design and the pace of construction, while the community around you is planned with a clubhouse, gardens, courts and paths.

Plot sizes will be announced at launch. Each plot will then have an area, a shape and a place in the master plan: on a corner or mid-street, facing a garden or another row of homes. Plots of the same area can feel very different, so weigh the position as closely as the size.

Ask at launch whether the layout sets design guidelines for the homes built on its plots, such as limits on height or on the look of the front, and whether construction has to start by a certain date.

Prestige Bannerghatta Road independent villas

An independent villa is a house on its own plot, designed and built by Prestige. It is a finished home: you choose a villa type from the configurations on offer, and Prestige builds it and hands it over.

Villa configurations and areas will be announced at launch. The artistic impressions in the gallery suggest the character planned for them, such as a villa veranda opening onto a planted courtyard and an independent villa behind a planted arrival court. They are impressions, not plans, so wait for the villa plans before judging rooms and sizes.

Plot area and built-up area at Prestige Bannerghatta Road

Plots and villas are measured differently. A plot is sold on its plot area, the land within its boundaries. A villa has a plot area too, and also the floor area of the house built on it.

For the house, carpet area is the usable floor area inside the walls, the space you can actually furnish. Built-up area adds the thickness of the walls, and super built-up area adds a share of common spaces as well. Ask which of these the villa plans and the price list use, so that you compare like with like.

For a plot, check the unit too. Plot areas can be stated in square feet, square yards or square metres, and the plans, the price list and the cost sheet should all use the same one.

Reading the Prestige Bannerghatta Road plot and villa plans

When the plans come out at launch, read each one with these questions in mind:

  • Which way does the plot or villa face, and where is the north arrow?
  • How wide is the road in front, and is it a through road or a quiet street?
  • Is it a corner plot, and does a corner carry a premium?
  • What is next to it: a garden, the clubhouse, a path or another home?
  • For a villa, how many bedrooms are there, and where do the bedrooms, living spaces and parking sit?
  • Are the dimensions marked, and do they match the area on the cost sheet?

The same size in a different position can suit a different family, so mark your choices on the master plan, not just on the price list.

Plot or villa: which suits you at Prestige Bannerghatta Road

A plot suits buyers who want to design their own home, or to build later or in stages as their needs and budget allow. The cost of the house sits on top of the plot price, and so do the time and effort of building it.

A villa suits buyers who want a finished home with the design settled, one price and one handover, and who would rather not manage construction themselves.

Both sit in the same planned community, with the same clubhouse, pools, courts, gardens and paths, so the choice is about how you want to own rather than where.
